# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Canaster is for non-technical white-collar and blue-collar professionals who need to plan, organize, inspect, and update work visually without thinking in databases, code, or diagramming syntax. They may be coordinating field work, office operations, service delivery, projects, assets, or recurring processes, and they need the canvas to make relationships, status, and next actions clear at a glance.

## Product Purpose

Canaster is a nested visual canvas workspace for building and navigating working documents. It lets users arrange information into canvases, move between parent and child contexts, and persist documents through the Daptin backend. Success means users can capture a messy real-world workflow, zoom into the right level of detail, return to the bigger picture without losing orientation, and trust that their work is saved.

## Brand Personality

Calm, powerful, grounded. The interface should feel approachable to people who do not identify as technical, while still making complex work feel controllable. Precision matters operationally: selection, position, hierarchy, navigation, sync state, and affordances should be unambiguous, but the product voice should not feel clinical or engineering-first.

## Anti-references

Do not make this feel like a developer diagramming tool, a BI dashboard, a generic whiteboard, or a novelty mind-map app. Avoid terminal aesthetics, engineering jargon, dense admin-console chrome, overplayful collaboration cues, ornamental gradients, decorative canvas flourishes, and interfaces that assume the user already understands graph or database concepts.

## Design Principles

Make orientation obvious: users should always understand where they are, what is selected, what is inside the current canvas, and how to get back to the parent context.

Keep power quiet: advanced canvas behavior should be available through familiar controls and direct manipulation, not through intimidating panels or technical vocabulary.

Represent work as work: nodes, documents, and nested canvases should read as practical objects people can act on, not abstract graph primitives.

Favor trust over spectacle: save state, loading, errors, disabled actions, and destructive transitions must be visible, plain, and recoverable.

Support mixed environments: layouts and controls should remain usable for people switching between desk work, field interruptions, shared screens, and touch devices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for text contrast, focus visibility, keyboard access, and non-pointer workflows. Reduced motion support is required for canvas transitions and UI feedback. Important states must not rely on color alone; canvas selection, sync status, disabled actions, and errors need shape, text, or icon reinforcement. Touch targets and dense toolbar controls must remain usable for people in interrupted or mobile work contexts.
