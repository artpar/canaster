## Overall Impression

The bones are good: Canaster has a disciplined visual system and a serious canvas atmosphere. The biggest opportunity is to turn the first screen from an engine workbench into a practical work surface. Right now the UI proves the canvas engine is capable; it does not yet reassure a non-technical professional that this is for their work.

## What's Working

- The visual system is restrained and coherent: compact controls, 6-8px radii, functional accents, and stable overlay chrome are all good product choices.
- Selection and manipulation feedback have a real foundation: blue strokes, focus outlines, resize handles, breadcrumbs, and status feedback are already present.
- The dark/light theme pairing avoids most decorative product clichés and supports a long-session tool surface.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] First-run orientation is broken**

**Why it matters**: The first visible canvas content is partially obscured by chrome and dominated by internal sample concepts. Users cannot tell what they are supposed to do first, whether the sample is editable, or how nested canvases relate to their work.

**Fix**: Fit the initial workspace below the toolbar, ensure starter nodes are visible, replace engineering sample content with a real operational example, and add one subtle first-run affordance near the canvas rather than a modal.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable onboard src/App.tsx`

**[P1] Product vocabulary leaks implementation**

**Why it matters**: The target audience is non-technical. Terms like `Canvas Renderer`, `accent task`, coordinates, `Canvas portal`, `Drawn 5/5`, and child IDs make the product feel like a developer diagramming tool.

**Fix**: Rename visible concepts around work: "Work item", "Inside", "Open details", "Move to related plan", "Saved locally", "Synced". Hide coordinates, dimensions, render counts, and internal IDs behind an advanced details mode.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable clarify src/App.tsx`

**[P2] Topbar IA is overloaded**

**Why it matters**: Canvas controls, account creation, document state, theme switching, and node panel access compete equally. The canvas loses hierarchy to chrome before the user understands the workspace.

**Fix**: Split account/document controls into a compact document/account menu or right-side popover. Keep the persistent topbar focused on canvas navigation, document safety, and one obvious way to access work items.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable distill src/App.tsx`

**[P2] Node panel behaves like an inspector, not a guide**

**Why it matters**: It should help users understand and act on work objects, but currently emphasizes geometry, internal type metadata, and abstract canvas structure.

**Fix**: Convert rows into work-object summaries: title, human type, short description, state, and primary action. Move technical metadata into a collapsible "Details" section.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable layout src/App.tsx`

**[P2] Mobile/touch experience is not task-usable**

**Why it matters**: The product explicitly targets mixed environments and field interruptions. At narrow width, toolbar, auth fields, node panel, and statusbar consume the viewport while the canvas becomes secondary.

**Fix**: Collapse auth into an account button, use bottom-sheet behavior for node/work-item details, and preserve canvas visibility as the invariant. Keep one visible canvas action row, not the full desktop chrome.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable adapt src/App.tsx`

## Minor Observations

- The brand mark is simple and effective, but the workspace content has less product confidence than the app name.
- The statusbar exposes rendering metrics before user-facing state.
- Placeholder copy uses lowercase `email` and `password`, which feels unfinished next to the otherwise precise UI.
- Light theme is readable, but some dark filled action buttons in panels feel heavier than their importance.
- Reduced-motion support exists through `data-animation='off'` for stack plane transitions, but there is no broader CSS `prefers-reduced-motion` policy visible in the stylesheet.
- Browser detector flagged flat type hierarchy; this aligns with the human finding that chrome, auth, node metadata, and status all compete in the same small range.

## Questions to Consider

- What would the starter canvas look like if it were built for a facilities manager, service dispatcher, or site supervisor rather than the canvas engine author?
- Why is authentication visible before the user understands the value of the workspace?
- Should "nested canvas" be a visible concept, or should users simply feel like they can open a work item and come back?
- What belongs in the default work surface, and what belongs in an advanced inspector?
- If the app had only one sentence to reassure users their work is safe, would `Local draft` be it?
